## What the 4 Series is

BMW introduced the 4 Series in 2013 as a way to give its two-door cars their own identity. Until then, the coupe and convertible were sold as part of the 3 Series (the E92 coupe, for example). Splitting them off gave BMW room to make the cars lower, wider, and more stylish than the sedan they were built on. Underneath, the 4 Series shares a lot with the 3 Series of the same era, which is why the two have such similar chassis codes (more on that in [[4-series-generations|4 Series Generations and Chassis Codes]]).

### Body styles

The first-generation 4 Series came in three versions:

- **Coupe (F32):** the classic two-door, and the one most people picture.
- **Convertible (F33):** the same car with a folding hardtop roof.
- **Gran Coupe (F36):** a four-door with a sloping roofline and a hatchback-style rear opening, so it carries more than the coupe does.

## My car: a 2016 440i Gran Coupe

Mine is a 2016 440i Gran Coupe in Mineral Grey Metallic. It's powered by BMW's 3.0-liter turbocharged inline-six, the B58, which makes roughly 320 horsepower and 330 lb-ft of torque (the full story on that engine is in [[engines-n54-n55-b58|BMW Turbo Inline-6 Engines]]). The 440i replaced the 435i around this time, and it was one of the first cars to get the newer B58 engine.

I've had this car since I was 16, and it now has about 90,000 miles on it. That's a lot of time behind one steering wheel, and it's a big part of why I care so much about BMW.

> What I keep coming back to is how the car drives. It feels sporty and connected in a way that makes even a boring commute more fun.

### Why the Gran Coupe works for me

The Gran Coupe doesn't have the pure looks of the two-door, but it gives me the extra doors and a usable trunk without feeling like a family sedan. For a daily driver that I also want to enjoy, that trade-off made sense. It also means that friends can ride in the back without climbing over a seat.
