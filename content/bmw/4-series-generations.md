## How to read a chassis code

BMW enthusiasts rarely say "2016 4 Series" when they can say "F36." A chassis code is a short name for a specific generation of a car, and it's the fastest way to talk about exactly which version you mean. The letter shows the era (E for the 1980s through the early 2010s, F for roughly the 2010s, G for the late 2010s onward), and the number identifies the body style. When you hear "E92 M3" or "F82 M4," those are the names that matter, much more than the model year.

## First generation: F-series (2013–2020)

The first 4 Series was built on the same platform as the F30 3 Series. The coupe came first, and the other body styles followed.

| Code | Body | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| F32 | Coupe | The original two-door |
| F33 | Convertible | Folding hardtop |
| F36 | Gran Coupe | Four-door with a hatchback tailgate (my car) |
| F82 | M4 Coupe | Twin-turbo S55 engine |

### What changed over the years

In 2016 the 435i's N55 engine gave way to the B58 in the 440i, which is why a 2016 440i is an interesting year to own (see [[engines-n54-n55-b58|BMW Turbo Inline-6 Engines]]). BMW also gave the car a mid-generation refresh, which helps explain why earlier and later cars look slightly different.

## Second generation: G-series (2020 onward)

BMW launched the redesigned 4 Series coupe in 2020, along with the G23 convertible, and the G26 Gran Coupe followed in 2021. The most obvious change is the huge vertical kidney grille, which people either love or really don't. The electric BMW i4 uses the G26 Gran Coupe body, so the 4 Series name now covers both gas and electric cars.

#### A quick timeline

1. 2013: the 4 Series coupe launches, replacing the 3 Series coupe.
2. 2014: the F82 M4 arrives, and the convertible and Gran Coupe follow.
3. 2016: the 440i replaces the 435i with the B58 engine.
4. 2020–2021: the G22 generation arrives, including the G82 M4.
