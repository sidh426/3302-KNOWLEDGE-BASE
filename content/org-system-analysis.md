# Organizing System Analysis: Wikipedia's Car Articles

**Author:** Sid Bhardwaj
**Course:** CMPA 3302
**Collection analyzed:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/), focusing on its automobile articles

---

## Why This Collection

Since my knowledge base is about sports cars, Wikipedia's car coverage was a natural pick. It's usually my first stop when I want to look up a model's history, generations, or specs. To keep things concrete, I used the **BMW 4 Series** (the car I drive) as my main example.

---

## 1. Primary Resources

The primary resources are **articles**, and in the car space they come in a few layers:

- **Model articles** that cover a whole nameplate, like *BMW 4 Series*
- **Generation articles** that cover one version of a model, like *BMW 4 Series (F32)* and *BMW 4 Series (G22)*
- **Variant articles** for special versions, like *BMW M4*

Each article is backed by supporting resources such as photos (many pulled from Wikimedia Commons) and a **References** section linking to outside sources like car magazines and BMW press releases.

---

## 2. Primary Interactions

Wikipedia supports a few main interactions:

- **Research:** looking up production years, engines, dimensions, and design history
- **Browsing:** clicking from one car to the next, which is honestly how I lose an hour
- **Fact-checking:** following footnotes to the original source
- **Comparing:** jumping between generations to see what changed

> The infobox at the top of each article is the fastest way in. In a few seconds I can see the manufacturer, production years, body style, and class without reading a single paragraph.

---

## 3. Classification System

Wikipedia actually uses **both** systems.

### Hierarchical

Car articles follow a clear "is-a" structure:

`BMW` → `BMW 4 Series` → `BMW 4 Series (G22)` → `BMW M4`

The main 4 Series article acts as a parent that summarizes each generation and points down to the more detailed child articles.

### Faceted

At the bottom of each page, articles are tagged with **multiple categories**, such as the car's class, body style, and the year it was introduced. Because one article can sit in many categories at once, readers can approach the same car from different angles. That works a lot like facets.

### Is It Effective?

For its audience (curious readers, students, and enthusiasts), mostly yes. The hierarchy makes it easy to drill down, and categories help with browsing. The weak spot is that categories are hidden at the very bottom of the page, so casual readers rarely use them.

---

## 4. Relationships Between Resources

Wikipedia makes relationships really explicit:

| Relationship | How It Shows Up | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Lineage | Infobox **Predecessor** / **Successor** fields | F32 is succeeded by the G22 |
| Related models | Infobox **Related** field | 4 Series is related to the 3 Series |
| Part-of | Links from parent to generation articles | 4 Series → F32, G22 |
| Reference | Inline hyperlinks and footnotes | Links to press releases and reviews |
| Grouping | Categories and navigation boxes | BMW model timeline at the bottom |

The **Predecessor** and **Successor** fields stand out to me most. They turn a pile of separate articles into a timeline you can walk through car by car.

---

## Takeaways

Wikipedia's car articles combine a clear hierarchy with flexible, category-based grouping, and link everything together with structured relationships. It's a good model for my own knowledge base. Using consistent fields like predecessor and successor in my car notes would make my site much easier to explore.
